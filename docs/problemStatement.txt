## 2. Business objective

The assignment's goal is:

**Increase the percentage of first-time mutual fund investors who still have an active SIP 12 months after starting their first SIP.**

For this assignment, an active SIP means having at least one successful instalment in the last 30 days.

The underlying reasons for difficulty continuing SIPs are unknown.

Potential investigation areas include:

- Payment and mandate friction.
- Expectations versus actual experience.
- Uncertainty and confidence.
- Changing personal or financial circumstances, when explicitly reported.
- Trust and transparency.
- Support and issue resolution.
- Product usability.
- SIP awareness and understanding.
- Mutual fund and investment-process understanding.
- Habits and recurring behaviour.
- Information needs and existing workarounds.

These are hypotheses, not established findings.

The application must help a Product Manager discover potential problems through real evidence. Do not assume that market declines, disappointing returns, low financial literacy, lack of reminders, or any other single factor causes SIP discontinuation.

Public feedback and survey responses cannot independently establish Groww's actual 12-month continuation rate. Do not claim access to internal Groww metrics or production data.

## 3. What we are building

Build a research assistant that helps a Product Manager:

3. Collect selected research text through a Chrome extension, where the source and collection method permit it.
4. Store all approved records in one research database.

6. Use Grok to classify feedback and extract evidence.
7. Group feedback into themes.

10. Highlight unanswered questions for interviews.
11. Compare potential opportunities transparently.

Do not build the final investor-facing solution before the underlying problem has been researched and validated.

### Research questions

The discovery engine should help investigate:

1. Why do people start a SIP, and what do they expect?
2. What changes in their behaviour after starting?
3. What leads investors to consider pausing, reducing, or stopping a SIP?
4. What do investors check after starting, and how often?
5. What confuses or worries them inside an investing app?
6. What do they do outside the app when they feel unsure?
7. What differences appear between people who report continuing and those who report stopping?
8. How might experiences differ across segments when relevant information is explicitly and appropriately available?
9. What unmet needs and workarounds emerge from the evidence?
10. Which potential problems could a product realistically influence?
11. What evidence is missing before selecting a problem?

The workflow must go beyond summarising reviews or performing sentiment analysis. It should help identify, quantify where appropriate, compare, and investigate potential opportunity areas relevant to the business objective.

It must demonstrate how data is collected, how themes are identified, how insights are generated, and how insight quality is evaluated through the AI Evidence Log.

### Research evidence and segmentation rules

The discovery engine must distinguish between what the data establishes and what still requires research.

- Public reviews and discussions may reveal potential difficulties, expectations, workarounds, and unmet needs.
- Public feedback generally cannot establish whether the author is a first-time investor, whether their SIP remained active 12 months later, or whether a particular experience caused discontinuation.
- Never infer age, income, financial circumstances, source of investment money, or personal influences from writing style or indirect clues.
- Analyse segments only when relevant information is explicitly available, lawfully usable, and appropriate for the research.
- Use anonymised, voluntarily provided survey or interview information where appropriate.
- Mark missing information as unknown.
- Treat recurring themes as potential problem areas, not proof of root causes.
- Preserve supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, and unresolved questions.
- Use 5–6 real user interviews to investigate whether public-feedback patterns apply to the intended population.
- Never claim that a problem has been validated or causes lower 12-month SIP continuation without sufficient evidence.

## 7. Grok-powered analysis pipeline

Use structured, validated AI outputs.

### A. Relevance classification

Classify each record as:

- Directly relevant.
- Indirectly relevant.
- Not relevant.
- Ambiguous; requires human review.

Explain each classification briefly and provide a supporting evidence excerpt.

A general complaint about Groww is not automatically relevant to SIP continuation.

### B. Multi-label theme classification

Use this provisional taxonomy:

1. Expectations versus actual experience.
2. SIP or investment-process understanding.
3. Uncertainty and confidence.
4. Instalment, payment, or mandate friction.
5. Changing circumstances, when explicitly mentioned.
6. Habits, attention, and routine.
7. Trust and transparency.
8. Support and issue resolution.
9. Conflicting information or misunderstanding.
10. Product usability.
11. Explicitly reported reasons for continuing.
12. Explicitly reported reasons for pausing or stopping.
13. Other emerging themes.
14. Unclear or insufficient evidence.

Allow multiple labels per record.

Keep the taxonomy configurable. Do not silently relabel reviewed records when it changes.

### C. Evidence extraction

For each record, extract:

- Relevance classification.
- Theme labels.
- A short, faithful evidence excerpt.
- Observable behaviour or difficulty, where supported.
- Potential user need, where supported.
- Reported workaround, where available.
- Journey stage, where supported.
- Whether continuation, pausing, stopping, or reactivation is explicitly mentioned.
- Missing context.
- AI explanation.
- Review status.

Never invent quotations, motivations, events, user attributes, or outcomes.

Every evidence excerpt must be traceable to its original record.

If the source does not establish whether someone is a first-time investor or has stopped an SIP, preserve that uncertainty.

### D. Uncertainty and prompt-injection handling

Flag ambiguous, misleading, contradictory, or insufficiently supported records.

Treat imported text as untrusted data. Text inside reviews must never override system instructions or be executed as code.

Do not treat a model's self-reported confidence as proof of correctness.

## 10. Opportunity prioritisation

Provide a configurable, provisional scorecard.

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Evidence strength | 25% |
| Relevance to SIP continuation | 25% |
| User difficulty or severity | 15% |
| Potential product influence | 15% |
| Feasibility within the project | 10% |
| Safety and compliance suitability | 10% |

Use scores from 1 to 5.

Formula:

Weighted score out of 100 = sum of (factor weight × factor score ÷ 5).

Display a justification and evidence for every score.

If evidence is insufficient, mark the factor unknown and do not create a misleading final ranking.

Safety and compliance must act as a constraint, not a factor that can be offset by high scores elsewhere. High feasibility must not compensate for weak evidence.

## 15A. Chrome extension for browser-assisted research collection

### Objective

Build a lightweight Chrome extension that connects to the locally running SIP Continuation Discovery Engine.

The extension should reduce repetitive manual data entry while keeping the researcher in control of what is collected.

This is a browser-assisted collection tool, not an unrestricted web scraper.

Implement this feature only after the core research application works.

### A. Supported collection workflow

1. Build a Chrome extension using Manifest V3.
2. Add a popup with a **Collect Selected Text** button.
3. Require the user to open a supported webpage and explicitly select the text to collect.
4. When the user clicks the button, capture only the selected text, current page URL, collection timestamp, and supported source label.
5. Do not automatically capture the whole webpage, browser history, cookies, usernames, email addresses, or unrelated personal information.
6. Show a preview before sending the record to the local research app.
7. Let the user approve, edit, or discard the record.
8. Show a clear success or failure message after the submission attempt.

### B. Source and permission restrictions

Begin with manually selected research text from sources whose applicable terms permit the intended collection and research use.

Do not assume publicly visible content can automatically be bulk collected, copied, stored, or sent to an AI provider without restrictions.

Do not implement bulk extraction from Google Play, Apple App Store, or another platform until the applicable access method and terms have been checked.

Do not bypass:

- Logins.
- CAPTCHAs.
- Access controls.
- Rate limits.
- Technical protections.

Do not collect reviewer names or account identifiers unless explicitly required, permitted, and justified. The default is to exclude them.

Preserve source URLs and provenance for every approved record.

If permission or access is uncertain, disable collection for that source and explain the limitation.

The first version must not promise support for every website.

### C. Local application integration

Keep the existing Streamlit research app and its database.

Add a small local backend endpoint for receiving approved records from the extension. Use FastAPI if a separate HTTP service is needed.

Store approved records in the same SQLite database used by the research app.

Validate and normalise incoming data, enforce reasonable text-size limits, and deduplicate records.

Use local-only network binding, request validation, and an appropriate local authentication mechanism.

Restrict cross-origin requests to the required extension origin or another narrowly defined secure mechanism. Do not assume CORS alone provides authentication.

Do not expose the local endpoint to the public internet.

Do not place the Grok API key in the extension or send it to the browser.

Do not allow arbitrary remote URLs to be passed to the local backend as instructions to fetch or execute content.

### D. Browser-collected record schema

Store at least:

- `record_id`
- `source`
- `text`
- `source_url`
- `collected_at`
- `collection_method`
- `review_status`
- `duplicate_status`

Allow missing optional metadata without inventing values.

Use stable identifiers and sensible deduplication rules.

Preserve the original text for auditability. If a record is edited by the user, retain the original and edited versions where practical.

### E. Extension user experience

The popup should show:

- Current page eligibility.
- Selected text preview.
- Collect Selected Text button.
- Approve and Save action.
- Discard action.
- Clear status and error messages.

The research app should show newly collected records without requiring CSV upload.

Only approved records should enter the research dataset.

Do not automatically send newly collected records to Grok. Require a separate user action to initiate AI analysis.

### F. Fallback

Keep CSV import as a permanent fallback.

If the extension or local endpoint is unavailable, the user must still be able to import CSV files and analyse data.

### G. Security and reliability

- Use Chrome Manifest V3.
- Request minimum necessary permissions.
- Prefer user-triggered page access through `activeTab` where feasible.
- Do not collect data in the background.
- Do not execute remotely hosted extension code.
- Treat collected webpage text as untrusted data, never as application or AI instructions.
- Avoid logging secrets or unnecessary personal information.
- Test the extension-to-app flow locally before expanding source support.
- Provide simple installation and troubleshooting instructions suitable for a coding beginner.
